Why 200 clicks became 4 visitors

  • Not every click was a visit attempt. LinkedIn's standard "Clicks" metric counts every click on an ad, including clicks on the company name, logo, "see more" text, and social actions, so a large share of the 200 were never attempts to visit the page.
  • Low intent from the audience. The clicks that did head to the page came from an audience that mostly scrolled past the ad (a 0.02% CTR), which signals curiosity at best, not buying intent.

Ad clicks by Job Title

The 12 top job titles by clicks. Remaining titles are grouped as "Other."

Manager 17 clicks, Project Manager 12, CEO 12, Operations Manager 10, Office Manager 9, Sales Manager 6, CFO 5, COO 5, Owner 4, Founder 4, President 4, District Manager 4, other 108.
  • CFOs and COOs clicked at 0.04%, double the rate of Managers, Project Managers, and CEOs at 0.02%.
  • Manager and Project Manager absorbed about 19% of impressions. Project Manager is a weak fit for an executive assistant buyer and is the clearest place to cut.
  • District Manager posted the best CTR at 0.08%, though on a small base of 4,888 impressions.

Ad clicks by Industry

The 12 top industries by clicks. Remaining industries are grouped as "Other."

Technology 32 clicks, Business Consulting 20, IT Services 15, Hospitality 14, Media and Telecom 14, Medical Practices 13, Food and Beverage 10, Non-profit 10, Construction 9, Wholesale 9, Transportation and Logistics 9, Retail 8, other 37.
  • Business Consulting and Services took the largest share of impressions at 14.5% but returned 10% of clicks at a 0.01% CTR. Construction shows the same pattern.
  • Technology, Information and Internet delivered 16% of clicks from under 11% of impressions. With IT Services and Media and Telecommunications, the tech cluster produced just over 30% of all clicks.
  • Hospitality, Wholesale, and Transportation and Logistics all held a 0.03% CTR, above the campaign average.

Ad clicks by Location

The 8 top metro areas by clicks. These metros account for only 22% of clicks and about 18% of impressions.

New York City 15 clicks, Montreal 6, Chicago 5, Toronto 4, Denver 4, Charlotte 4, Philadelphia 3, Detroit 3, other 156.
  • Greater Toronto is the weakest metro, with 4.25% of impressions, 2% of clicks, and a 0.01% CTR.
  • Greater Montreal earned more clicks than Toronto from a third of the impressions. Charlotte (0.05%) and Denver (0.04%) are small but efficient.
  • More than 80% of impressions landed outside the top metros, a sign that geographic targeting is broad enough to spread the budget thin.

Action items for the next run

Grouped by when they need to happen. Items in the first group should be completed before any new budget goes live.

Before relaunch: fix the form

  • Fix the intro copy. Correct "How much of the week goes on work that doesn't need?" to read "work that doesn't need you."Done when the intro screen reads cleanly.
  • Time screens 2 and 3 against the one-minute promise. Complete the audit on a phone with a stopwatch and note how long each screen takes. Any screen that runs well past 10 seconds needs trimming.Done when all six screens fit comfortably inside a minute on mobile.
  • Make the time estimates effortless. Wherever screens 2 and 3 ask executives to estimate hours, use preset ranges or tap-to-select options instead of typed numbers or fine-grained sliders, and cut any question that does not change the final result.Done when every answer on those screens takes a single tap.
  • Show a running tally mid-form. After screen 2 or 3, give a preview of the result, such as "So far, about X hours a week could come off your plate." This rewards the effort at the exact point people leave and delivers on the "your hours, counted" promise early.Done when visitors see a first number before screen 4.
  • Check where contact details are requested. If name and email only appear on the final screen, move a light email capture earlier, framed as "Where should we send your results?", so anyone who drops later can be sent a link to finish.Done when partial completions can be followed up.
  • Build a condensed variant. Create a version with screens 2 and 3 merged and trimmed, ready to split test against the current six-screen form.Done when both versions are live-ready with per-screen completion tracking.

Before relaunch: reset the campaign setup

  • Change the optimization goal. Set the campaign to optimize for landing page clicks or website visits instead of general engagement.Done when the campaign objective matches the audit page goal.
  • Set up a Lead Gen Form test. Build a native LinkedIn version of the shortened audit to run alongside the landing page.Done when both paths are ready to compare on completions.
  • Create the retargeting audience. Build a matched audience from Time Audit page visitors so it starts filling from day one.Done when the audience is live in Campaign Manager.

Before relaunch: tighten targeting

  • Cut weak job titles. Exclude Project Manager and reduce broad Manager and Sales Manager reach.Done when these titles no longer take a leading share of impressions.
  • Prioritize senior decision makers. Focus on CFO, COO, Owner, Founder, President, and Operations Manager.Done when the title list reflects the buyers who clicked at the highest rates.
  • Rebalance industries. Exclude or down-bid Business Consulting and Construction, and prioritize the technology cluster, Hospitality, Wholesale, and Transportation and Logistics.Done when industry exclusions and bid adjustments are applied.
  • Narrow geography. Pause or rework Toronto, keep New York and Montreal, and shift test budget toward Charlotte and Denver.Done when the location list is focused rather than broad.

Before relaunch: refresh the creative

  • Write outcome-led headlines. Build new variations around hours reclaimed each week rather than the service itself.Done when at least three new hooks are ready to test.
  • Preview the audit result in the ad. Show a sample result in the same format the audit returns: hours a week, days a year, and the type of Executive Assistant recommended.Done when at least one creative variant features the sample result.
  • Match the ad to the page. Carry the page's own promises into the ad copy: "get back 30+ hours a week" and "six screens, about a minute, no card, no obligation."Done when a visitor sees the same promise in the ad and on arrival.

During the run: test, monitor, and follow up

  • Run the form split test. Send traffic evenly to the current and condensed form versions.Watch completion rate at each screen, especially screen 2 to 3 and screen 3 to 4.
  • Compare landing page and Lead Gen Form paths. Track completed audits from each route.Watch cost per completed audit once spend data is available.
  • Launch retargeting to abandoners. Serve the matched audience an ad built around unfinished progress, such as "Your hours are half counted. Finish your audit in under a minute."Watch how many return visitors finish the form.
  • Check the click-to-visit gap weekly. Compare LinkedIn landing page clicks with GA4 users on the audit page.Watch for the gap narrowing after the setup changes.
  • Review audience breakdowns at the midpoint. Re-pull job title, industry, and location data and cut any segment still underperforming.Watch for new weak spots before the budget is spent.
